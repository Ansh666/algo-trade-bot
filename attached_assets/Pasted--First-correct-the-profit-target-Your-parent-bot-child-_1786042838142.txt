## First, correct the profit target

Your **parent bot + child bots** architecture is absolutely buildable. However:

* Investing ₹100 and exiting at ₹110 means a **10% return**, not 2–3%.
* Earning 2% every trading day for 250 days would mathematically multiply capital by about **141×**.
* Earning 3% daily would multiply it by about **1,619×**.

That shows why 2–3% **every day** is not a realistic expectation. Some days the bot will profit, some days it will lose, and many days it should take **no trade**. SEBI’s study found that 7 out of 10 individual equity-cash intraday traders made losses. ([Securities and Exchange Board of India][1])

Use this safer objective:

```text
Daily profit cap:       +1% or +2%
Daily maximum loss:     -0.5%
Risk per trade:         0.10%–0.25%
Maximum trades:         2–4
Risk-to-reward:         Minimum 1:2
```

Here, `+2%` is the point where the bot **stops trading for the day after earning it**. It is not a guarantee that the bot will earn 2% every day.

# 1. Parent-bot and child-bot architecture

Do not build multiple independent AI systems that can all place orders. Build one **Parent Trading Controller**, with specialist child modules.

```text
                    PARENT BOT
             Trading Session Controller
                         │
       ┌─────────────────┼───────────────────┐
       │                 │                   │
 Market Scanner     Risk Manager       Account Manager
       │                 │                   │
 Data Collector    Position Sizing      Funds/Settlement
       │                 │
 Strategy Bots ─── Risk Approval
       │                 │
       └────────── Execution Bot
                         │
                  Position Manager
                         │
               Stop-loss / Target /
                  Trailing Stop
                         │
                 Reconciliation Bot
                         │
                 Reports and Alerts
```

## Parent bot

The Parent Bot controls the entire trading day.

Its responsibilities:

```text
1. Authenticate with the broker.
2. Confirm WebSocket and database connectivity.
3. Check available funds.
4. Confirm that no old position is open.
5. Start all child bots.
6. Permit or reject new trades.
7. Monitor daily profit and daily loss.
8. Stop all trading when a limit is reached.
9. Close intraday positions before market closure.
10. Generate the final daily report.
```

Only the Parent Bot should be permitted to send an approved instruction to the Execution Bot.

# 2. Child bots

## A. Universe Selection Bot

Do not simply select the 20 largest companies.

Select 20 stocks from an approved liquid universe such as large and actively traded NSE shares using:

* Average daily turnover
* Average volume
* Bid-ask spread
* Recent volatility
* Sufficient movement for intraday trading
* No circuit restriction
* No unusual liquidity problems
* No duplicate highly correlated positions

Example:

```text
Approved universe: 50 liquid shares
             ↓
Liquidity screening
             ↓
Remove high-spread shares
             ↓
Remove abnormal or restricted shares
             ↓
Select top 20 candidates
```

You should manually define the initial approved universe. Do not allow an AI model to select unknown or illiquid penny stocks.

---

## B. Market Data Bot

This bot collects:

* Live price
* Bid and ask
* Volume
* Open, high, low and close
* 1-minute candles
* 5-minute candles
* Previous-day high and low
* VWAP
* Nifty movement
* Order and trade updates

Broker WebSockets should be used instead of repeatedly calling REST APIs. Kite Connect supports real-time WebSocket market data, while Upstox also provides streaming market and portfolio/order updates. ([Kite Trade][2])

Store completed candles, but do not save every raw tick indefinitely during the MVP.

## C. Indicator Bot

This child calculates deterministic features:

```text
Relative volume
VWAP
20 EMA
50 EMA
ATR
Recent swing high
Recent swing low
Support levels
Resistance levels
Opening range
Previous-day high and low
Price momentum
Volume-price relationship
```

Initially, calculate support and resistance using rules—not a language model.

For example:

```text
Resistance:
A price area rejected at least twice recently

Support:
A price area where price previously bounced

Breakout:
Price closes above resistance,
volume exceeds normal volume,
and spread remains acceptable
```

## D. Strategy Bots

Build only **one or two strategies** in 20 days.

### Strategy 1: Breakout with volume

```text
Stock above VWAP
20 EMA above 50 EMA
Price breaks recent resistance
Current volume > 1.5 × normal volume
Nifty direction is not strongly opposite
Minimum reward-to-risk = 2
```

### Strategy 2: Pullback to support

```text
Stock is in an uptrend
Price returns near VWAP/support
Selling volume reduces
Bullish confirmation candle appears
Stop-loss can be placed below the support
Minimum reward-to-risk = 2
```

Do not add candlestick patterns, news sentiment, machine learning, support/resistance, order-book imbalance and ten indicators simultaneously. That makes it impossible to identify why the strategy succeeds or fails.

## E. Signal Ranking Bot

The bot may analyse 20 stocks but should not trade all 20.

Example scoring system:

```text
Liquidity quality              15 points
Relative volume                20 points
Trend agreement                15 points
Support/resistance quality     15 points
Breakout confirmation          15 points
Nifty/sector alignment         10 points
Acceptable spread               5 points
Reward-to-risk                  5 points
                              ─────────
Maximum                       100 points
```

Trading rule:

```text
Score below 75: Reject
Score 75–84: Watch
Score 85 or more: Send to Risk Bot
```

The scoring weights must later be validated through backtesting.

# 3. Risk Bot—the most important child

The Risk Bot must have **veto authority**. Neither the Parent Bot nor the AI strategy should be able to override it.

The Risk Bot checks:

```text
Is the daily loss limit reached?
Is the daily profit cap reached?
Are too many positions already open?
Is this stock already being traded?
Is the spread too large?
Is the market data stale?
Is the stop-loss valid?
Is the target realistically reachable?
Is sufficient balance available?
Is the position correlated with existing positions?
Does the trade provide at least 1:2 reward-to-risk?
```

## Quantity calculation

Never purchase a fixed quantity simply because the share costs ₹200.

Use:

```text
Quantity =
Maximum money allowed to lose
÷
Difference between entry and stop-loss
```

Example with ₹20,000 capital:

```text
Capital:                 ₹20,000
Risk per trade:          0.25%
Maximum planned risk:    ₹50

Entry:                   ₹200
Stop-loss:               ₹198
Risk per share:          ₹2

Quantity:                ₹50 ÷ ₹2
Quantity:                25 shares

Position value:          ₹5,000
Target at 1:2:           ₹204
```

Possible gross outcome:

```text
Stop-loss:
25 × ₹2 = ₹50 planned loss

Target:
25 × ₹4 = ₹100 planned profit
```

Actual results can differ because of charges and slippage.

# 4. Order Execution Bot

Use a standard interface so you can change brokers later:

```python
class BrokerAdapter:
    def place_order(self, order):
        raise NotImplementedError

    def modify_order(self, order_id, changes):
        raise NotImplementedError

    def cancel_order(self, order_id):
        raise NotImplementedError

    def get_order(self, order_id):
        raise NotImplementedError

    def get_positions(self):
        raise NotImplementedError

    def get_available_funds(self):
        raise NotImplementedError
```

Then implement:

```text
ZerodhaBrokerAdapter
or
UpstoxBrokerAdapter
```

Kite Connect provides order placement, modification, cancellation, market data and portfolio APIs. Its current Connect tier lists real-time WebSocket and historical candle data at ₹500 per month. ([Kite Trade][3])

Upstox provides a sandbox for placing, modifying and cancelling test orders without real funds, making it particularly useful during development. ([Upstox - Online Stock and Share Trading][4])

## Correct order sequence

```text
1. Risk Bot approves trade.
2. Execution Bot sends entry order.
3. Broker returns order ID.
4. Bot waits for the actual fill update.
5. Bot reads filled quantity and average price.
6. Bot immediately places stop-loss for filled quantity.
7. Bot confirms that stop-loss order is active.
8. Position Manager starts monitoring the position.
```

Never assume that receiving an `order_id` means the order was executed.

The order may be:

```text
OPEN
PARTIALLY FILLED
COMPLETE
REJECTED
CANCELLED
```

Your database state should be:

```text
CANDIDATE
    ↓
SIGNAL_CREATED
    ↓
RISK_APPROVED
    ↓
ENTRY_SUBMITTED
    ↓
ENTRY_PARTIALLY_FILLED / ENTRY_FILLED
    ↓
STOP_LOSS_SUBMITTED
    ↓
PROTECTED_POSITION
    ↓
EXIT_SUBMITTED
    ↓
CLOSED
```

If the entry fills but the protective stop cannot be confirmed, the bot must invoke an emergency exit using the broker-permitted protected order type.

# 5. Stop-loss and trailing-stop process

Example:

```text
Entry:            ₹200
Initial stop:     ₹198
Initial risk:     ₹2 per share
Target:           ₹204
```

Here:

```text
1R = ₹2
2R = ₹4
```

Trailing process:

```text
Price reaches ₹202:
Trade has achieved +1R.
Move stop near break-even only after accounting for normal movement.

Price reaches ₹203:
Trail stop below the latest confirmed swing or candle structure.

Price reaches ₹204:
Exit at the 2R target.
```

Better logic:

```python
if current_profit_r >= 1.0:
    protect_break_even()

if current_profit_r >= 1.5:
    trail_below_recent_swing()

if current_profit_r >= 2.0:
    exit_position(reason="target_reached")
```

Do not modify the stop on every single price tick. That will create too many broker requests and cause unnecessary exits.

# 6. Daily account controller

Use four separate values:

```text
available_cash
used_margin
realized_profit
unsettled_profit
withdrawable_balance
```

Example daily rules:

```python
DAILY_PROFIT_CAP_PERCENT = 1.0
DAILY_LOSS_LIMIT_PERCENT = 0.5
RISK_PER_TRADE_PERCENT = 0.25
MAX_TRADES_PER_DAY = 3
MAX_OPEN_POSITIONS = 2
```

Parent Bot logic:

```python
if daily_realized_pnl <= -daily_loss_limit:
    close_all_positions()
    cancel_all_orders()
    disable_new_trades("daily_loss_limit")

elif daily_realized_pnl >= daily_profit_cap:
    close_all_positions()
    cancel_all_orders()
    disable_new_trades("daily_profit_cap")
```

Reaching the profit cap should stop new trades. The bot should not continue trading because it “sees one more opportunity.”

# 7. Profit cannot be immediately deposited into your bank

After an intraday trade is closed:

```text
Trade closes
    ↓
Profit appears as realised/unsettled profit
    ↓
Exchange settlement completes
    ↓
Profit becomes withdrawable
    ↓
You submit a broker withdrawal request
    ↓
Money reaches the bank
```

For example, Zerodha states that intraday profit normally becomes available after T+1 settlement; it is not immediately available for withdrawal or reuse as fresh trading profit on the same day. ([Zerodha Support][5])

Therefore, create a **Settlement Bot**, but do not assume it can directly transfer money to your bank.

Its job should be:

```text
Read broker ledger
Identify unsettled funds
Identify withdrawable funds
Record settlement status
Send withdrawal reminder
Never count unsettled profit as available capital
```

Automatic bank withdrawal should only be implemented if your chosen broker provides an official and permitted payout API. Do not automate browser clicks or store broker passwords.

# 13. Regulatory and infrastructure requirements

The current retail algo framework requires broker-controlled API access and includes provisions around static IP mapping, risk controls, order-rate thresholds, tagging and audit trails. NSE’s standards describe mandatory static-IP mapping and an initial threshold of no more than 10 orders per second before higher-speed registration requirements apply. 

SEBI’s implementation glide path stated that the framework would apply to all stockbrokers from April 1, 2026. Broker-specific onboarding and implementation should therefore be confirmed with your chosen broker before live deployment. 

Use:

```text
Ubuntu VPS
Static public IP
HTTPS
Encrypted environment variables
OAuth-based broker authentication
Daily access-token refresh
Restricted firewall
Database backups
Complete audit logs
```

For example, Kite’s access token expires at 6 AM the following day, so its session must be authenticated for the trading day. Do not store your broker password or automate 2FA using insecure scripts. ([Kite Trade][6])

# 14. What counts as success after 20 days

Not:

```text
The bot earned 2% today.
Therefore it is successful.
```

Success means:

```text
No duplicate order
No unprotected position
Every order is reconciled
Every position has a confirmed stop
Daily loss limit works
Bot stops after the profit cap
Disconnected data prevents new trades
Backtest includes realistic costs
All actions have audit logs
Emergency exit works
```

After completing the 20-day MVP, run it in paper mode for at least **4–8 weeks** across different market conditions. Begin live testing with one share or the smallest practical quantity, then increase only after the live results match the tested behaviour.

The correct first version is therefore:

> **A rule-based, multi-module trading system with AI-assisted stock ranking—not an AI with unrestricted control over your money.**
