## Yes, the idea makes sense—with one important correction

A **60-day MVP** can be built that:

* Analyses 20–25 liquid shares.
* Selects up to 10 valid opportunities.
* Uses historical candles, live volume, support, resistance, trend and volatility.
* Buys only after all conditions pass.
* Places a stop-loss immediately after the entry is filled.
* Exits around a small predefined target.
* Moves the stop-loss upward when the position becomes profitable.
* Returns the released amount to the app’s available balance display.
* Stops trading after reaching a daily profit or loss limit.

But after 60 days, you should expect a **working paper-trading application**, not a system proven to make daily profits.

# 1. Correct the ₹200 example

Suppose one share is purchased at:

```text
Entry price: ₹200
Target price: ₹204
```

Your potential gross profit is:

```text
₹204 - ₹200 = ₹4
```

If you want a **1:2 risk-to-reward ratio**, your maximum planned risk should be ₹2:

```text
Entry:     ₹200
Stop-loss: ₹198
Target:    ₹204

Risk:      ₹2
Reward:    ₹4
Ratio:     1:2
```

For a ₹205 target:

```text
Entry:     ₹200
Stop-loss: ₹197.50
Target:    ₹205

Risk:      ₹2.50
Reward:    ₹5
Ratio:     1:2
```

Therefore, when you say “loss should be only ₹4,” the stop-loss would approximately be:

```text
Entry:     ₹200
Stop-loss: ₹196
Target:    ₹204

Risk:      ₹4
Reward:    ₹4
Ratio:     1:1
```

So the best configuration for your objective is closer to:

```text
Entry: ₹200
Maximum planned loss: ₹2
Target profit: ₹4
```

The loss can still exceed ₹2 during sudden gaps, poor liquidity or fast price movement. A stop-loss controls planned risk but does not guarantee the exact execution price. Upstox’s current API, for example, supports market, limit, stop-loss-limit and stop-loss-market orders, along with market-protection parameters. ([Upstox - Online Stock and Share Trading][1])

# 2. Ten stocks require separate capital

If the bot purchases one ₹200 share in each of ten companies:

```text
₹200 × 10 stocks = ₹2,000 total exposure
```

You should also calculate total open risk:

```text
Risk per position: ₹2
Ten positions:     ₹20 planned open risk
```

But ten stocks can fall together because many stocks move with Nifty, Bank Nifty or their industry sector.

A safer rule is:

```text
Analyse:                 20–25 stocks
Qualified opportunities: 0–10
Maximum open positions:   3–5
Maximum stocks per sector: 1–2
```

The system must not force itself to purchase ten stocks every day. Some days only two stocks may qualify; on other days none should qualify.

# 3. Do not hold user money inside your application

This is the biggest architectural decision.

## Wrong design

```text
User deposits ₹10,000 into your application
Your application holds the money
Your application invests it
Your application later returns the money
```

This creates serious custody, payment, brokerage and investment-management issues.

## Recommended design

```text
User’s bank
     ↓
User’s registered broker account
     ↓
Your application receives limited API authorisation
     ↓
Orders execute directly in that user’s broker account
```

Your application should contain a **virtual wallet ledger**, not the actual money.

The app wallet should only display:

```text
Broker available cash
Amount allocated to bot
Amount reserved in positions
Unrealised profit/loss
Realised but unsettled profit
Settled balance
Withdrawable balance
```

The broker remains the source of truth.

Client funds are subject to strict segregation and handling requirements, and current SEBI rules require brokers to treat client funds separately from their own funds. ([Securities and Exchange Board of India][2])

# 4. How the virtual application wallet works

Suppose the user has ₹10,000 in their broker account but permits your bot to use only ₹2,000.

```text
Broker balance:           ₹10,000
Bot-authorised allocation: ₹2,000
App wallet available:      ₹2,000
```

The bot purchases one share for ₹200:

```text
Available: ₹2,000
Reserved:  ₹0
```

After the order fills:

```text
Available: ₹1,800
Reserved:  ₹200
```

The position exits at ₹204:

```text
Original amount released: ₹200
Gross profit:               ₹4
```

The application ledger becomes:

```text
Available principal:       ₹2,000
Realised unsettled profit:     ₹4
Displayed total:           ₹2,004
```

However, the app must not immediately mark the ₹4 as withdrawable. Equity and F&O trade settlement generally follows T+1, and Zerodha states that intraday profits become available for trading or withdrawal after settlement on the next trading day. ([Zerodha Support][3])

Therefore, your wallet model should have these balances:

```python
available_balance
reserved_balance
unrealised_pnl
realised_unsettled_pnl
settled_balance
withdrawable_balance
```

Never use only one `wallet_balance` field.

# 5. User permission system

Before the bot starts, the user should create a trading mandate.

Example:

```json
{
  "allocated_capital": 2000,
  "maximum_position_value": 200,
  "maximum_open_positions": 5,
  "maximum_trades_per_day": 10,
  "risk_per_trade": 2,
  "daily_maximum_loss": 20,
  "daily_profit_cap": 40,
  "minimum_reward_risk_ratio": 2,
  "auto_trading_enabled": true
}
```

The permission screen should clearly display:

```text
Maximum capital usable: ₹2,000
Maximum one-stock position: ₹200
Maximum planned loss per trade: ₹2
Maximum daily planned loss: ₹20
Target per completed trade: approximately ₹4
Maximum simultaneous positions: 5
Permission valid until: today’s market close
```

The user must be able to:

* Pause new trades.
* Close every position.
* Revoke broker access.
* Reduce capital limits.
* Disable a particular strategy.
* View every decision and order.
* Disable automatic trading completely.

Use broker OAuth or official token authentication. Do not ask users to give you their broker password, PIN or OTP.

# 6. Parent bot and child bots

Your parent bot should control the complete trading session.

```text
                        PARENT BOT
                   Session Orchestrator
                            │
       ┌────────────────────┼─────────────────────┐
       │                    │                     │
 Permission Bot       Market Safety Bot      Wallet Bot
       │                    │                     │
 Universe Bot         Risk Manager           Broker Funds
       │                    │                     │
 History Bot          Position Sizer          Settlement
       │                    │
 Live Data Bot        Trade Approval
       │                    │
 Indicator Bot ─────── Signal Ranking
       │                    │
 Strategy Bots ─────── Execution Bot
                            │
                    Position Manager
                            │
                Stop / Target / Trailing
                            │
                   Reconciliation Bot
```

## Parent Bot

The Parent Bot should:

1. Authenticate the broker account.
2. Read the user’s latest permissions.
3. Fetch available broker funds.
4. Confirm market-data connectivity.
5. Confirm that no unknown position already exists.
6. Start the market scanner.
7. Receive signals from strategy bots.
8. Send signals to the Risk Bot.
9. Permit only approved orders.
10. Stop the complete system after the daily limit.
11. Close intraday positions before market close.
12. Reconcile application records against broker records.

The Parent Bot should not directly calculate support or resistance. Its purpose is orchestration and safety.

# 7. Child-bot responsibilities

## Universe Bot

Starts with an approved list, such as liquid large-cap shares.

It removes stocks with:

* Poor volume.
* Large bid-ask spreads.
* Abnormal price behaviour.
* Trading restrictions.
* Insufficient historical information.
* Excessive correlation with already selected stocks.

Output:

```text
Approved daily universe: 20–25 stocks
```

## Historical Research Bot

Fetches:

* Previous 60–250 trading days.
* One-minute and five-minute candles.
* Average daily volume.
* Average intraday range.
* Previous support and resistance zones.
* Opening gaps.
* Previous-day high and low.
* Historical breakout success rate.

It should update data before the market and after market closure, not recalculate several years of history on every tick.

## Live Volume Bot

Calculates:

```text
Current five-minute volume
Average five-minute volume
Relative volume
Price movement with volume
VWAP position
Volume acceleration
```

Example:

```text
Current volume: 30,000
Normal volume at this time: 15,000
Relative volume: 2.0
```

## Support and Resistance Bot

Creates price zones instead of one exact number.

Example:

```text
Support zone:    ₹197.80–₹198.20
Resistance zone: ₹201.80–₹202.10
```

It can use:

* Recent swing highs and lows.
* Previous-day levels.
* Repeated price rejection.
* Volume concentration.
* Opening range.
* ATR-based tolerance.

## Pattern Bot

Initially detect only a few patterns:

```text
Volume breakout
Pullback to VWAP
Higher-high continuation
Support bounce
Resistance rejection
```

Do not add 30 candlestick patterns in the first version.

## Ranking Bot

Scores each candidate:

```text
Liquidity:                 15
Relative volume:           20
Trend agreement:           15
Support/resistance quality:15
Breakout confirmation:     15
Index/sector alignment:    10
Reward-to-risk:            10
                           ──
Total:                    100
```

Example rule:

```text
Below 75: Reject
75–84: Watch only
85+: Send to Risk Bot
```

# 8. The Risk Bot must have final authority

The Risk Bot should reject the trade when:

* The maximum allocated capital is reached.
* Daily loss limit is reached.
* Daily profit cap is reached.
* Too many positions are open.
* The same stock is already open.
* Too many stocks from the same sector are open.
* Market data is delayed.
* Stop-loss is too close.
* Bid-ask spread is excessive.
* Reward-to-risk is below 1:2.
* Broker funds are insufficient.
* The market is approaching intraday closing time.

Example approval:

```text
Stock: ABC
Expected entry: ₹200
Stop-loss: ₹198
Target: ₹204
Quantity: 1
Position value: ₹200
Planned risk: ₹2
Potential reward: ₹4
Risk/reward: 1:2
Decision: Approved
```

# 9. Exact order process

The bot must not place all orders blindly at once.

## Step 1: Submit entry

```text
BUY 1 share near ₹200
```

## Step 2: Wait for broker confirmation

The order may become:

```text
Submitted
Open
Partially filled
Completely filled
Rejected
Cancelled
```

Receiving an order ID does not mean the share has been purchased.

## Step 3: Read actual average fill price

Suppose the expected entry was ₹200 but the actual fill was:

```text
Actual entry: ₹200.20
```

The bot should recalculate the stop and target from the actual entry or verify that the original technical levels still produce an acceptable ratio.

## Step 4: Place protective stop immediately

Example:

```text
Actual entry: ₹200.20
Stop trigger: ₹198.20
Target:       ₹204.20
```

## Step 5: Confirm that protection is active

Only then should the database mark:

```text
POSITION_PROTECTED
```

If the stop-loss order is rejected, the Emergency Bot must close the position or repeatedly attempt an approved protective exit.

Official broker APIs support order placement, modification and cancellation; Upstox also documents target and stop-loss GTT structures and trailing-stop functionality. ([Upstox - Online Stock and Share Trading][4])

# 10. Target and trailing-stop logic

Example:

```text
Entry: ₹200
Initial stop: ₹198
Target: ₹204
```

Possible process:

```text
Price below ₹202:
Keep initial stop at ₹198

Price reaches ₹202:
Trade reaches 1R profit
Move stop carefully toward ₹200

Price reaches ₹203:
Trail stop to approximately ₹201

Price reaches ₹204:
Exit at target
```

A safer implementation should use a state machine:

```text
INITIAL_RISK
     ↓
ONE_R_REACHED
     ↓
BREAK_EVEN_PROTECTED
     ↓
TRAILING_ACTIVE
     ↓
TARGET_REACHED
     ↓
CLOSED
```

Do not move the stop-loss on every ₹0.05 movement. Normal market noise will repeatedly close positions prematurely.

Use either:

* Fixed step trailing.
* Recent candle low.
* Swing-low trailing.
* ATR-based trailing.

For the first version, use fixed-step or swing-low trailing because it is easier to test.

# 11. Handling target and stop together

When both target and stop-loss orders exist, one must be cancelled after the other executes.

Example:

```text
Target order executed
       ↓
Cancel stop-loss order
       ↓
Confirm cancellation
       ↓
Mark position CLOSED
```

Or:

```text
Stop-loss executed
       ↓
Cancel target order
       ↓
Confirm cancellation
       ↓
Mark position CLOSED
```

Without this, the second order could later execute and accidentally create a short position.

Your reconciliation process should compare:

```text
Application position quantity
Broker position quantity
Open target quantity
Open stop-loss quantity
```

Any mismatch should disable new trading immediately.

# 12. Daily trading flow

## Before market

```text
08:30–09:00
Download latest historical data
Update approved instrument list
Calculate major support/resistance
Verify broker authentication
Verify static IP and server health
Load user permissions
```

## Market opening

```text
09:15–09:30
Observe only
Build opening range
No automatic entry
```

## Trading period

```text
09:30 onwards
Analyse 20–25 stocks
Rank candidates
Submit only qualifying signals
Maximum 3–5 simultaneous positions
```

## Profit/loss control

Example with ₹2,000 authorised capital:

```text
Daily profit cap: ₹40
Daily loss limit: ₹20
```

When realised profit reaches ₹40:

```text
Cancel pending entries
Close or safely manage existing positions
Disable new trading
Mark session PROFIT_LOCKED
```

When realised loss reaches ₹20:

```text
Cancel pending orders
Close open positions
Disable new trading
Mark session LOSS_LOCKED
```

## End of day

```text
Stop new entries before closing time
Close all intraday positions
Cancel remaining orders
Reconcile broker and application
Generate daily report
Update wallet ledger
```

# 17. Personal use versus other users

## Personal application

For only your own broker account, this is considerably simpler:

```text
Your broker account
Your funds
Your API permission
Your strategy
Your risk limits
```

You still must follow your broker’s API and current retail-algo requirements.

## Application for multiple users

When other users connect their accounts and your system automatically decides and executes trades, it becomes much more regulated.

SEBI’s retail-algo framework routes such activity through brokers, and NSE currently maintains an empanelment framework for algo providers. The NSE page was updated on July 31, 2026 and refers algo providers to the April 30, 2026 empanelment circular. ([Securities and Exchange Board of India][6])

If your service independently chooses investments and manages other people’s funds, investment-advisory or discretionary portfolio-management questions may also arise. SEBI describes securities investment advice as falling under the Investment Adviser framework, while discretionary portfolio management is a separately registered activity with a ₹50 lakh minimum client investment requirement. You should obtain specialist securities-law advice before allowing public users or charging them. ([Securities and Exchange Board of India][7])

Therefore:

```text
Phase 1: Personal account only
Phase 2: Each user’s own broker account, no custody
Phase 3: Broker partnership and regulatory review
Phase 4: Public commercial product
```

Do not start by accepting money into your company’s application wallet.

## Final practical design

Your concept should be implemented as:

```text
User authorises ₹2,000
        ↓
Money remains in user’s broker account
        ↓
Parent Bot analyses 20–25 stocks
        ↓
Risk Bot permits 0–5 positions
        ↓
Each position may use approximately ₹200
        ↓
Entry is confirmed
        ↓
Stop-loss is activated immediately
        ↓
Target is placed around 2R
        ↓
Trailing stop protects increasing profit
        ↓
Position closes
        ↓
Application releases reserved capital
        ↓
Profit appears as realised-unsettled
        ↓
After broker settlement, wallet shows settled balance
```

This makes technical and financial sense. The correct objective is not “every trade will make ₹4.” It is:

> Every trade has a controlled planned loss, a larger potential profit, complete execution protection, and the system takes no trade when conditions are poor.
